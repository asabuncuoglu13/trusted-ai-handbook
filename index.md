# Trusted AI Handbook

I've been researching and working on the responsible development and use of AI systems for over eight years now. The ultimate goal of this book is to make my job prefix, _Responsible AI_, obsolete.

In most engineering disciplines, a "Responsible X" specialisation isn't common. We don't see job adverts with _responsible mechanical engineer_ title, right? Because, an engineer should follow standard engineering safety protocols, engineering ethics, and best-practices which are defined by their professionl bodies. However, computer science disciplines are young, and AI is younger. Professional standards and ethics are still actively discussed and formed.

This handbook is a humble attempt to help bringing best practices together in one place, so that practitioners at any stage of their professional journey can find the essentials in a single, practical reference.

## Organising the book

Trust is multifaceted, and building it requires collaboration across disciplines. Organising the knowledge so it is both digestible for readers and maintainable for authors is therefore challenging. To provide a practical starting point, the book is organised into three parts: Organise, Operationalise, and Observe.

1. **Organise:** Deciding how to structure this book was difficult. Similarly, organising the knowledge, relationships, and accountabilities involved in the design, development, maintenance, and governance of AI systems is challenging. The first section focuses on conceptual understanding and clarifying roles to enable effective management and governance.
2. **Operationalise:** Turning this conceptual understanding into practice requires translating principles into process, tooling, and decision-making. This section focuses on the operational controls, workflows, and engineering practices needed to design, build, test, deploy, and maintain AI systems responsibly across their lifecycle.
3. **Observe:** Trust is not a one-time achievement; it must be continuously verified and improved. This section focuses on monitoring, evaluation, incident response, and feedback loops so teams can detect drift, risks, and harms after deployment and act before they escalate.
